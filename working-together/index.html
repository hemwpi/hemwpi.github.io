Prospective Students

I welcome inquiries from prospective students interested in research in biomechanics, motor learning, and the design and control of assistive robotic devices. I am specifically recruiting PhD students to begin in Fall 2027 and Master's students year-round.

Students in the lab will contribute to a growing research program while developing as independent researchers and preparing for their future careers. Mentorship will include developing research questions, building technical, writing, and presentation skills, and learning how to carry a project forward with increasing independence.

Relevant backgrounds may include biomedical, mechanical, or robotics engineering; rehabilitation science; kinesiology; and computational neuroscience. Prior experience with hardware and control systems is especially relevant to device-focused work, while simulation and machine learning are assets for modeling-focused projects. Students should expect to develop skills in quantitative analysis, experimental research with human participants, and collaboration across disciplines. I look for students who are prepared to acquire the skills and methods their research requires, including those beyond their prior experience.

I would also be glad to hear from undergraduate students interested in a sustained research experience or a capstone project. If any of these describe you, please send a note describing your background and interests.

Collaborators

Understanding how people move and developing technologies that respond to people’s mobility needs is a collaborative endeavor. I welcome collaborators who contribute to different parts of this work, including:

Clinical expertise: Physical therapists, orthotists and prosthetists, physiatrists, and other clinicians working with aging, physical rehabilitation, gait and neurological conditions.

Disability expertise: Disabled people, assistive technology users, disability justice scholars, and advocates whose knowledge can shape the questions we ask and the technologies we pursue.

Design expertise: Product and interaction designers, and human factors researchers focused on how devices are understood, used, and incorporated into daily life.

Computational & engineering expertise: Researchers in biomechanics, robotics, control systems, simulation, machine learning, and related areas interested in understanding human movement or developing adaptive assistive technologies.

If you work on related questions or see a connection with your own work, I would be glad to hear from you.
